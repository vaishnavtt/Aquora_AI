## 1. Executive Summary

This document presents a comprehensive, empirical audit of the datasets currently present in the AQUORA AI repository, identifies critical variable gaps across three operational domains (water-level forecasting, environmental hazard assessment, and future drowning-incident risk research), evaluates official government data sources for Kerala, and provides a prioritized, methodologically sound data acquisition roadmap.

### Core Scientific Principles Observed:
- **Zero Hallucination:** No fictional sensors, fabricated incident statistics, or synthetic credentials are assumed.
- **Preservation of Pipeline:** The active production models, training pipeline, and feature engineering scripts remain completely untouched.
- **Empirical Rigor:** Additional data inputs are evaluated based on physical hydrological mechanics. We do not claim additional data automatically improves predictive accuracy without rigorous out-of-time cross-validation.

## 4. Domain-Specific Missing Data Analysis

### Part A: River Water-Level Forecasting

The current forecasting model (`RidgeRegressor`, test MAE: `1.04 cm`, RMSE: `1.45 cm`) operates strictly as an autoregressive kinematic delta model over a **1-hour horizon** ($t+1$). 

#### Missing Hydrological Predictors:
1. **Upstream Catchment Rainfall:**
   - *Physical Mechanism:* River stage at Ramamangalam Bridge (Muvattupuzha River basin) is governed by runoff generated in the Western Ghats headwaters (Idukki and Ernakulam district border catchments).
   - *Forecasting Limitation:* Over longer lead times (e.g., 3h, 6h, 12h, 24h), autoregression loses predictive skill. Without precipitation inputs, sudden flood waves resulting from upstream cloudbursts cannot be anticipated until the surge physically reaches the water-level sensor.
2. **Upstream Dam & Reservoir Releases:**
   - *Physical Mechanism:* The Muvattupuzha basin receives regulated discharge from the Malankara Dam (Muvattupuzha Valley Irrigation Project) and tailrace discharges from the Moolamattom powerhouse (Idukki Hydroelectric Project).
   - *Forecasting Limitation:* Gate openings cause step-function stage increases that purely statistical lag models perceive as unannounced anomalies. Dam outflow schedules are necessary for multi-hour flood stage anticipation.
3. **Upstream Tributary Stage Telemetry:**
   - The Muvattupuzha River is formed by the confluence of the Thodupuzha, Kaliyar, and Kothamangalam rivers. Upstream stage monitoring at tributary junctions would provide wave propagation lead times of 2 to 6 hours.

---

### Part B: Environmental Hazard Assessment

The platform's `BaselineHeuristicPredictor` estimates danger levels using flow rate ($\text{m}^3/\text{s}$), water temperature (°C), 24h rainfall (mm), and wind speed (km/h). In the active application, these variables are currently simulated or manually adjusted via what-if sliders.

#### Missing Hazard Assessment Variables:
1. **Volumetric Discharge / Flow Velocity ($v$ in $\text{m/s}$, $Q$ in $\text{m}^3/\text{s}$):**
   - High water stage does not uniquely map to dangerous kinetic energy. A shallow mountain stream with $3.0\text{ m/s}$ current is far more hazardous to a human than a $5.0\text{ m}$ deep, slow-moving backwater pool. Flow velocity is required to compute hydrodynamic dragging and toppling forces.
2. **Water Temperature & Cold Shock:**
   - Tropical rivers in Kerala generally range between $22^\circ\text{C}$ and $30^\circ\text{C}$, but deep reservoir release conduits (e.g. bottom sluices) or high-altitude streams can plunge below $15^\circ\text{C}$, inducing sudden respiratory gasping and cold shock. Real temperature telemetry is required if cold shock warnings are retained.
3. **Turbidity & Underwater Obstacles:**
   - Suspended sediment during monsoon surges eliminates water transparency ($<0.3\text{ m}$ Secchi depth), obscuring submerged rocks, tree trunks, and hydraulic boil vortexes.

---

### Part C: Future Drowning-Incident Risk Research

A critical objective for future research is understanding drowning risks without falling into pseudo-scientific correlation.

#### Required Incident Data Dimensions:
1. **Incident Geolocation & Spatial Context:**
   - Precise coordinates or micro-location (e.g., specific river bend, weir downstream, bridge piling, waterfall plunge pool, unmonitored bathing ghat).
2. **Exact Timestamp & Environmental Alignment:**
   - Date and time of incident, enabling alignment with historical river stage, flow velocity, and weather conditions at the moment of the event.
3. **Incident Typology & Causative Factor:**
   - Recreational bathing/swimming vs. occupational fishing vs. flash flood entrapment vs. suicide vs. passenger ferry capsize.
4. **Human Exposure & Demographic Factors:**
   - Age, swimming proficiency, alcohol involvement, group dynamics, presence of warning signage, presence of trained lifeguards or rescue equipment.
5. **Ethical Safeguard Notice:**
   - *Warning:* Predicting "drowning risk" purely from hydrological stage without empirical human exposure and incident records is invalid. A river running at record flood stage in an unpopulated wilderness carries zero drowning risk if no humans enter the water. Drowning risk is a function of **Hazard $\times$ Exposure $\times$ Vulnerability**.

## 5. Investigation of Official Data Sources

The following official government portals and institutional archives were audited for suitability in Kerala:

### 1. Central Water Commission (CWC) / National Water Informatics Centre (NWIC)
- **Portals:**
  - India-WRIS: [https://indiawris.gov.in/](https://indiawris.gov.in/)
  - National Water Data Portal (NWDP): [https://nwdp.gov.in/](https://nwdp.gov.in/)
- **Agency:** Ministry of Jal Shakti, Government of India.
- **Available Variables:**
  - River stage (hourly / daily, metres).
  - River discharge ($\text{m}^3/\text{s}$ at designated CWC hydrological observation stations).
  - Rainfall from hydro-meteorological stations.
  - Reservoir storage, inflow, and outflow.
- **Geographic & Temporal Fit for Kerala:**
  - Covers major Kerala basins: Periyar, Bharathapuzha, Pamba, Chaliyar, Muvattupuzha, Achankovil.
  - CWC maintains regional hydrological stations across Kerala with continuous multi-year records.
- **Access Requirements & Licensing:**
  - Public open data under National Data Sharing and Accessibility Policy (NDSAP).
  - Batch download via web UI requires free registered user credentials.
  - REST API access requires institutional registration and API key provisioning through NWIC.

### 2. India Meteorological Department (IMD)
- **Portals:**
  - IMD Data Supply Portal: [https://dsp.imdpune.gov.in/](https://dsp.imdpune.gov.in/)
  - IMD Mausam Portal: [https://mausam.imd.gov.in/](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/)
- **Agency:** Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES), Government of India.
- **Available Variables:**
  - High-resolution gridded daily rainfall ($0.25^\circ \times 0.25^\circ$ spatial grid across India, 1901–present).
  - Automatic Weather Station (AWS) and Automatic Rain Gauge (ARG) sub-daily/hourly precipitation pings.
  - District-level weather forecasts and nowcasts.
- **Geographic & Temporal Fit for Kerala:**
  - High gauge density across all 14 Kerala districts.
  - Crucial upstream gauges in Idukki (e.g. Peerumedu, Munnar) and Ernakulam (e.g. Aluva, Neriamangalam, Piravom).
- **Access Requirements & Licensing:**
  - Gridded datasets are accessible for research and educational purposes upon registration.
  - Real-time/recent AWS data is published publicly on regional portals; bulk historical time series require formal academic requisition through NDC Pune.

### 3. Kerala State Disaster Management Authority (KSDMA) / SEOC
- **Portal:** [https://sdma.kerala.gov.in/](https://sdma.kerala.gov.in/)
- **Agency:** Government of Kerala.
- **Available Variables:**
  - Daily monsoon situation reports (statewide reservoir levels, river warnings, heavy rainfall logs).
  - Historical flood damage summaries and emergency response incident logs.
- **Geographic & Temporal Fit:**
  - 100% localized to Kerala at taluk/district levels.
- **Access Requirements:**
  - Publicly hosted daily PDF bulletins; structured historical data extraction requires document parsing or direct research request.

### 4. Kerala State Water Resources Department (Kerala SW) / Irrigation Department
- **Portal:** [http://www.irrigation.kerala.gov.in/](http://www.irrigation.kerala.gov.in/)
- **Agency:** Hydrology Directorate, Thiruvananthapuram.
- **Existing Asset Connection:**
  - Direct origin of the project's `rwl_tel_hr_kerala_sw_999_2021_2025.csv`.
- **Potential Extended Datasets:**
  - Stage-discharge rating equations ($Q = a(h - h_0)^b$) calibrated for the 26 monitoring stations.
  - Cross-sectional profile surveys and zero-gauge reference levels (RL).
- **Access Requirements:**
  - Formal academic data request or Right to Information (RTI) application to the Chief Engineer (Investigation & Design), IDRB, Thiruvananthapuram.

### 5. National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB) / Kerala Fire & Rescue Services (Drowning Data)
- **Portals:**
  - NCRB ADSI Reports: [https://ncrb.gov.in/](https://ncrb.gov.in/)
  - Kerala Fire and Rescue Services: [https://fire.kerala.gov.in/](https://fire.kerala.gov.in/)
- **Available Variables:**
  - Annual statistical summaries of accidental drowning fatalities broken down by state and broad cause categories.
- **Fit & Limitations:**
  - NCRB data is macro-aggregated (annual, district level). It does **not** contain timestamps, coordinates, or environmental sensor pairings.
  - Granular incident data (emergency call logs, rescue dispatches) is restricted to emergency services databases and requires formal ethical approval and institutional data-sharing agreements.

## 6. Data Quality, Compatibility & Methodological Concerns

Before integrating any additional datasets into the existing pipeline, the following engineering challenges must be addressed:

### A. Temporal Granularity Mismatch
- The river telemetry operates at **hourly / sub-hourly frequency**.
- If daily gridded rainfall (e.g., IMD $0.25^\circ$) is introduced, assigning a single daily figure uniformly across 24 hourly timestamps masks intense short-duration convective storms (e.g. $60\text{ mm}$ falling in 45 minutes vs. evenly spread over 24 hours). This temporal mismatch can degrade rather than improve model precision.
- *Resolution:* Only hourly precipitation (from AWS stations or hourly satellite/reanalysis products like ERA5-Land) is temporally compatible with 1-hour-ahead stage forecasting.

### B. Catchment Spatial Alignment
- Aligning rainfall at the gauge coordinates (`9.867° N, 76.517° E`) is physically insufficient.
- The catchment area draining into Ramamangalam Bridge extends over hundreds of square kilometers upstream into the Idukki hills. Water falling at the gauge flows away immediately, whereas water falling in the upper catchment takes hours to concentrate and arrive as a flood wave.
- *Resolution:* Precipitation must be computed as a catchment-weighted areal average using the delineated watershed boundary upstream of the gauge.

### C. The 540-Day Statewide Hiatus
- Any continuous external meteorological dataset (e.g., 2021–2025 IMD rainfall) will contain observations for the entire year 2023.
- However, the Kerala SW telemetry dataset has a complete statewide outage between **2022-11-25 15:00** and **2024-05-19 00:00**.
- *Resolution:* When merging external datasets, the non-interpolation rule must be strictly preserved: **Do not impute river water levels across the 540-day gap.** Feature-target pairs must be generated strictly within active continuous telemetry segments.

### D. Upstream Dam Control as a Confounder
- The Muvattupuzha River has regulated flows from Malankara Dam.
- If an ML model is trained on rainfall and stage without dam release data, it may observe heavy rainfall with zero river rise (if reservoir flood gates remain closed to store water), or sudden river surges with zero rainfall (if dam gates are opened for dry-season power generation or pre-monsoon drawdown).
- *Resolution:* Acknowledge dam regulation as an unobserved confounder until official dam gate telemetry is acquired.

## 7. Prioritized Acquisition & Integration Roadmap

To avoid speculative downloads and ensure methodological rigor, datasets should be acquired and integrated in four structured phases:

```
[Phase 1: Zero-Download] ──> [Phase 2: Hydrometeorology] ──> [Phase 3: Hydraulic Calibration] ──> [Phase 4: Incident Research]
Explore 25 Unused Stations    Catchment Hourly Rainfall       Stage-Discharge Rating Curves     Ethical Rescue Logs
Already on Disk (443k rows)   (IMD AWS / ERA5-Land)           (CWC / Kerala SW IDRB)            (KSDMA / Fire & Rescue)
```

### Phase 1: Capitalize on Unused Data Already Present on Disk (Immediate / Zero Overhead)
- **Objective:** Evaluate the other **25 Kerala river telemetry stations** already stored in `data/raw/rwl_tel_hr_kerala_sw_999_2021_2025.csv` (443,846 unused rows).
- **Key Candidate Stations:**
  - `MALAYATTOOR KODANAD BRIDGE` (Ernakulam District, Periyar River, 14,107 rows)
  - `PALA` (Kottayam District, Meenachil River, 15,771 rows)
  - `MANIYAR DAM` (Pathanamthitta District, Kakkad/Pamba River, 21,413 rows)
  - `Vanimal` & `Kadiyangad` (Kozhikode District, stations with confirmed discharge reporting, 18,980 rows)
- **Action Items:**
  1. Audit data quality, sampling consistency, and gap distributions for the top 5 stations.
  2. Test the existing Ridge regression delta architecture across these stations to verify spatial generalizability.
- **Dependencies:** None. Data is already verified and present in `data/raw/`.

### Phase 2: Upstream Catchment Hourly Rainfall Augmentation (Near-Term)
- **Objective:** Introduce real catchment rainfall to extend the forecasting lead time beyond 1 hour.
- **Recommended Data:**
  - Hourly precipitation from IMD Automatic Weather Stations in Idukki/Ernakulam or hourly gridded ERA5-Land reanalysis for bounding box `[9.5°N - 10.3°N, 76.4°E - 77.2°E]`.
- **Action Items:**
  1. Delineate the upstream catchment polygon for Ramamangalam Bridge using SRTM / MERIT-Hydro DEM.
  2. Extract hourly areal-averaged rainfall time series for 2021–2025.
  3. Align timestamps with the 91 continuous observation segments in `ramamangalam_hourly_features.csv`.
  4. Perform controlled ablation experiments comparing `Autoregressive Stage` vs. `Stage + Rainfall Lags` across lead times $t+1, t+3, t+6\text{ hours}$.

### Phase 3: Stage-Discharge Rating Curves & Riverbed Profiles (Mid-Term)
- **Objective:** Convert gauge water level (m) to physical volumetric discharge ($\text{m}^3/\text{s}$).
- **Target Source:** Kerala Irrigation Design & Research Board (IDRB) / CWC Hydrological Observation Stations.
- **Action Items:**
  1. Submit academic/RTI data request for calibrated rating curve equations ($Q = a(h - h_0)^b$) at Ramamangalam and Malayattoor.
  2. Validate whether calculated flow rates align with realistic seasonal hydrographs.
  3. Replace synthetic flow rates in the hazard scoring engine with physically derived discharge.

### Phase 4: Ethical Drowning Incident & Water Safety Records (Long-Term)
- **Objective:** Formulate an empirical, ethically sound water safety and rescue response model.
- **Target Agency:** Kerala Fire & Rescue Services / KSDMA.
- **Action Items:**
  1. Formulate formal institutional data-use protocols and secure institutional review/ethics clearance.
  2. Request anonymized water rescue dispatch logs (date, time, GPS coordinates, water body type, outcome).
  3. Intersect incident points with contemporaneous hydrological stage and weather records to identify empirical hydrodynamic hazard thresholds, moving beyond rule-based heuristics.
